# Executive AI Leverage Report

**Publisher:** Open Future Forum
**Research type:** Executive benchmark report (cross-functional operator-research)
**Audience:** C-suite executives across finance, security, marketing, and technology functions
**Edition:** Preview Edition
**Publication date:** July 2026
**Canonical source:** https://openfutureforum.com/research/executive-ai-leverage-report

## Overview

The Executive AI Leverage Report reads AI leverage across the executive suite as a whole, rather than a single function. It introduces the Executive AI Leverage Ladder, a three-stage framework describing how executives describe getting leverage from AI: productivity (AI as a thought partner), capability (AI as a co-worker completing whole units of work), and context (AI that understands the business well enough to ask its own questions). The report combines quantitative survey findings across finance, security, marketing, and founder rooms with qualitative practices drawn from Open Future Forum finance peer-learning sessions.

## Methodology & Sample

This is explicitly labeled a preview edition. Quantitative findings come from an instrument embedded in applications to seven Open Future Forum events in 2026, with 421 applicants answering at least one instrument question; figures below the program's 40-response floor are labeled directional. Because several questions allow multiple selections, some percentages are "any-mention" and can sum past 100%. Qualitative findings come from small, screened peer-learning sessions among finance leaders, reported as practices rather than statistics.

## Key Findings

- 71% of respondents at the largest finance event said their team already runs an AI tool, and 14 of 16 growth-lane respondents were past the exploration stage with AI agents — indicating broad deployment rather than early experimentation.
- Proving ROI was the top blocker to further AI spend (53% of finance respondents), and 62% expect a measurable return within six months, setting a short proof window for AI investments.
- 17% of finance respondents (roughly one in six) report funding AI directly from money that would otherwise have gone to headcount — offered as the first budget-level measurement of the "headcount leverage" thesis tracked across OFF's research.
- The CEO was the most-named signer of AI purchases (47%), with CFO/finance second (26%) — highlighting a gap between who holds AI purchasing authority and who is accountable for proving its return.
- Security leaders named securing AI agents and their access as their top concern (56%), but only 31% reported having a dedicated budget line for AI security.
- Among AI founders, pricing has shifted away from per-seat models: about half of charging founders price on usage and 18% on outcomes, while sales targets skew toward CIO/CTO (47%) and business units (35%), with finance named as buyer by only about 10%.
- A first vertical cut of founder data shows buying-door patterns differ by industry: enterprise software and infrastructure skews toward the CIO, fintech skews toward the business line, and healthcare is described as the slowest vertical to monetize AI.
